## Question 1: Entry to the EKL labs together with another person using one badge card is:

**Answer:** ✅ **Not allowed.**

**Explanation:** Each lab user must use their own valid badge — it is explicitly not allowed for more than one user to enter on one badge, and it is also not allowed to use someone else's badge.

**Source:** *1. EKL Introduction (December 2025).pdf* — section "Access to the EKL labs":
> "It is not allowed that more than 1 user enters a lab on one badge. Everyone must use their own badge. It is not allowed to use the badge of another person."

## Question 2: Which statements are correct about the EKL buddy system?

**Answer:** ✅
- **When working in wet benches or fume hoods a buddy is always required.**
- **Each lab user is responsible for finding his/her own buddy.**
- **Lab buddies must contact each other at least every 15 minutes, to check if everything is OK.**

**Explanation:**
- Any use of fume hoods or cleanroom wet benches requires a buddy "no matter what time of the day it is" — so it is NOT only an after-hours rule.
- The user is individually responsible for finding their buddy.
- Contact must be every 15 minutes.
- Buddies MAY change during lab use (so the "may not change" option is false).

**Source:** *1. EKL Introduction (December 2025).pdf* — section "EKL Buddy System / Buddy System rules":
> "Any use of fume hoods or cleanroom wet benches requires the use of the buddy system, no matter what time of the day it is."
> "Each lab user is responsible to find his/her own buddy."
> "Lab buddies must contact each other at least every 15 minutes, to check that all is well."
> "A buddy does not continuously have to be the same person and may change during lab use."

## Question 3: Safety glasses must be worn:

**Answer:** ✅ **When using the wet benches & fume hoods.**

**Explanation:** Safety glasses (or a protective face shield) must be worn when working with chemicals, which means whenever you use wet benches and fume hoods. They are not optional based on "having your own" or limited only to acids/bases.

**Source:** *3. EKL Safety Training (December 2025).pdf* — slide "Personal protection when working with chemicals":
> "Wear safety glasses. (If you already wear normal glasses, then over your normal glasses), or a protective face shield (also when you are looking over someone's shoulder)."

Also corroborated in *2024 Februari Handling chemicals in EKL.pdf*.

## Question 4: Select the cleanroom rule(s) that are TRUE.

**Answer:** ✅
- **Users must always clean up their working area before leaving.**
- **A user is not allowed to use more than three coloured process wafer boxes.**
- **Users must use coloured boxes and dedicated tweezers when working with "RED" metals.**
- **Once you are dressed in a cleanroom suit and have entered the cleanroom, you are not allowed to unzip the suit.**

**Explanation:**
- Eating/drinking is always forbidden — so option 1 is false.
- Shorts/sandals are never allowed (clothes are the only absorbent in a spill) — option 2 is false.

**Sources:**
- *Safety and behaviour rules.pdf* — "Never leave residues of chemicals when you are ready with your activities. Clean up everything and leave a clean workplace behind."
- *1. EKL Introduction (December 2025).pdf* — "A maximum of 3 coloured process boxes is allowed per cleanroom user."
- *2. EKL Contamination Control (December 2025).pdf* — "Use a proper coloured clean process box… Separate your non-contaminated tweezers from contaminated tweezers."
- *1. EKL Introduction (December 2025).pdf* — "Once you are dressed in a cleanroom suit and have entered the lab, you should not unzip the suit."

## Question 9: Put the corresponding meaning on the following MSDS picture (NFPA Hazard Diamond):

**Answer:** ✅
- **Top (red) = D — Fire hazard information**
- **Left (blue) = C — Health hazard information**
- **Right (yellow) = B — Reactivity information**
- **Bottom (white) = A — Specific hazard information**

**Explanation:** The NFPA 704 "Hazard Diamond" uses four colour-coded sections: red (top) for flammability, blue (left) for health, yellow (right) for reactivity, white (bottom) for special/specific hazards.

**Source:** *3. EKL Safety Training (December 2025).pdf* — slide "The Hazard Diamond":
> "Left (blue) = Health Hazard. Top (red) = Fire Hazard. Right (yellow) = Reactivity. Bottom (white) = Specific Hazard."

## Question 10: What do you need if your process involves the use of a "RED" metal?

**Answer:** ✅ **A process flowchart that complies with the EKL PCC document and Process Guide Table, and approval from the contamination officer.**

**Explanation:** Red metals (Ag, Au, Cu, Fe, Sb, Cr, Ni, Pt, Zn…) are CMOS-incompatible critical contaminants. The flowchart must comply with the PCC document + PGT, and needs contamination-officer approval. (Dedicated coloured boxes & tweezers are also used in practice but are not the *primary* required-approval item for starting the process.)

**Source:** *2024 Februari Handling chemicals in EKL.pdf*:
> "Do you use wafers, chemicals or materials that are not CMOS compatible? Then your flowchart must comply with the EKL PCC document and process guide table, and needs approval from the contamination officer."

Also *2. EKL Contamination Control (December 2025).pdf*:
> "When CMOS incompatible materials are going to be used, approval will also be needed from the EKL contamination officer (Hande Aydoğmus)."

## Question 16: When do you have to press the calamity button?

**Answer:** ✅
- **In case of a chemical spill larger than a few drops.**
- **If someone is exposed to a chemical liquid.**
- **If someone is exposed to a chemical fume.**

**Explanation:** The Calamity button (yellow) is for accidents involving people and chemical spills/exposures. Dropped empty glassware is not a chemical emergency. Cooling water leaks from a machine should trigger the equipment EMO, not the calamity button.

- *3. EKL Safety Training (December 2025).pdf* — "What to do in case of a chemical spill on a person: Press the CALAMITY button."
- *Safety and behaviour rules.pdf* — "Calamity button (yellow button): If an unknown liquid is on the floor, in case of chemical spills; If someone is exposed to chemical liquids; If someone is exposed to chemical fumes and/or gases."

## Question 17: What do you do when the fire or gas alarm sounds?

**Answer:** ✅ **You warn other users in your vicinity of the need to evacuate the lab, and exit the lab immediately.**

**Explanation:** Leave the work area immediately — do NOT finish your process, do NOT take off your cleanroom suit, do not investigate. Alert other users on the way out and head to the evacuation point.

**Source:** *3. EKL Safety Training (December 2025).pdf* — slide "What to do if there is an alarm":
> "Leave the work area immediately. Do not finish your process."
> "Alert other users on your way out."
> "Follow the escape route to the nearest emergency exit… Do NOT take off your cleanroom suit or lab coat."
> "Go to evacuation point 1 and log out."

## Question 18: When do you press an equipment EMO button?

**Answer:** ✅
- **If you suspect that a machine is not working properly and poses a threat to the lab or people in the lab.**
- **When cooling water is leaking out of the machine.**
- **If smoke is coming out of the machine.**

**Explanation:** The EMO (Emergency Machine Off) button cuts power to that machine only. It is meant for dangerous machine situations such as smoke, liquid leaks, or someone trapped inside the system. It does NOT trigger a cleanroom-wide alarm — for fire, press the Fire Alarm button instead. A wrong recipe is not an emergency.

**Source:** *3. EKL Safety Training (December 2025).pdf* — alarm button table:
> "EMO button — In case of a dangerous situation with a machine (e.g. smoke comes out, a person is trapped inside system, liquid is leaking)."
> Result: "Only for this tool: System will switch off… Warn the tool owner."

## Question 19: What do you do when you contaminate a tool?

**Answer:** ✅ **You turn the label on the tool to 'STOP', change the tool status in the NanoLabNL Information System to 'down', and inform the contamination officer.**

**Explanation:** Stop process → STOP-sign on tool → set status DOWN in NIS → notify staff/contamination officer. Do NOT call the Facility Team for tool contamination (they handle facilities, not tools). Do NOT continue working.

**Source:** *2. EKL Contamination Control (December 2025).pdf* — "User actions: Stop the process and put the machine down. (Label the tool with a STOP sign and report it down in the NanoLabNL Information system.) Inform the staff members that are responsible for the equipment. (Do not call the Facility Team for equipment contaminations…) Always inform the contamination officer."

Also *1. EKL Introduction (December 2025).pdf* — "The first step is to attach a STOP sign on the front of the tool. The second step is to change the machine status in NIS."

## Question 20: What do you do when you spot or cause a fire in any of the EKL labs?

**Answer:** ✅
- **You press the fire alarm button and leave the lab.**
- **You go to the evacuation point and wait for the Emergency Response Team (ERT) and/or fire department to arrive.**

**Explanation:** Press the fire alarm (this also alarms the ERT and the fire department — you do not need to call them yourself), evacuate the lab, then go to the evacuation point. Do NOT save notes, panic, take photos, or collect extinguishers — only attempt to extinguish a waste-basket-sized fire if it is safe.

**Source:** *3. EKL Safety Training (December 2025).pdf* — alarm button table:
> "Fire Alarm Button — In case of fire. An alarm sounds and the sign 'Cleanroom verlaten' lights up. The ERT is alarmed. The Fire Department is alarmed. All labs and basement must be evacuated."
> "If safe and possible (for waste basket size fires), try to extinguish the fire with a fire extinguisher. Leave the room immediately if you don't know how to use a fire extinguisher!"

And evacuation procedure: "Go to evacuation point 1 and log out. Wait for further instructions."

## Question 22: What is correct?

**Answer:** ✅
- **Users are allowed to clean up minor chemical spills (a few drops) by themselves.**
- **Fire extinguishers can only be used for fires smaller than a paper basket.**
- **It is not allowed to scratch your face with your gloves on.**

**Explanation:**
- Minor spill (a few drops): put on neoprene gloves, clean with a wet blue towel, dry with a new blue towel, inform Wet Bench team — this is a user-handled procedure.
- Fire extinguishers may only be used for waste-basket-sized fires; anything larger means evacuate.
- Cleanroom/neoprene gloves can be contaminated (chemicals on neoprene, particles/oils on nitrile) — you must not touch your body, face, doorknobs, phone, etc. with them.
- Eye-wash bottles are at every wet bench and fume hood, not specifically "near safety showers" — the better-supported answers are the other three.

**Sources:**
- *3. EKL Safety Training (December 2025).pdf* — "What to do in case of a minor chemical spill (a few drops): Put on neoprene gloves. Use a wet blue towel to clean up the drops…"
- *3. EKL Safety Training (December 2025).pdf* — Fire Alarm Button row: "If safe and possible (for waste basket size fires), try to extinguish the fire with a fire extinguisher."
- *2024 Februari Handling chemicals in EKL.pdf* — "Do not touch your body, clothing, doorknobs, phones, tablets, cleanroom notebooks, pens, equipment, etc. with the neoprene gloves. You can contaminate all the things you touch."

**Answer:** ✅ **Gas alarm, leave the cleanroom.**

**Explanation:** "verlaten" is the Dutch verb "to leave". This phrase appears on the alarm panels whenever an automatic gas detector triggers an alarm.

**Answer:** ✅
- **In the EKL reception hall at the main entrance (in front of office EKL00.010).**
